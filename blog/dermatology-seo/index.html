Dermatology SEO: Rank for Patient Searches

By John Blackwood, Founder · August 20, 2026 · 10 min read

Dermatology has a split personality that shapes everything about its SEO. The medical side (acne, eczema, skin checks) runs on physician referrals and insurance networks; patients mostly arrive already routed to you. The cosmetic side runs on search: nobody is referred to a laser resurfacing consult. They Google it, compare three practices, read reviews, and book the one that looks most credible.

That is why dermatology SEO, done right, is mostly cosmetic-revenue SEO, and why it’s one of the highest-leverage marketing investments a practice can make. Cash-pay treatments, high lifetime values, and a search landscape where most competitors are barely trying. Here’s the system.

The Searches That Actually Pay

SEO for dermatologists covers three families of searches, and they are not equally valuable:

  • Local practice searches: “dermatologist near me,” “dermatologist [city].” Highest volume, mixed medical/cosmetic intent, decided almost entirely by the local map pack.
  • Local treatment searches: “laser resurfacing [city],” “chemical peel near me,” “PRP hair treatment [city].” Lower volume, sharply commercial intent, cash-pay patients comparing providers. This is where the revenue lives.
  • Condition and question searches: “does microneedling help acne scars,” “tretinoin vs retinol.” Top-of-funnel, huge volume, and the raw material for the authority that lifts everything else.

Most practices have a homepage, one “Services” page, and nothing else, which means they compete for none of the treatment searches. That’s the gap you walk through.

Win the Local Pack First

For “dermatologist near me,” the map pack is the search result. The levers, in order of impact:

  • Google Business Profile done completely: primary category (Dermatologist), secondary categories for your cosmetic side (Skin care clinic, Medical spa where accurate), services listed individually, real photos of the practice and team added monthly.
  • Review velocity: a steady stream of new reviews outranks a big stale total. Build the ask into checkout, make the link one tap, and keep it consistent. One warning for medical practices: never confirm a reviewer is a patient in your replies. Thank them generically, take disputes offline. The reply that mentions a treatment is a privacy violation, not customer service.
  • NAP consistency: same name, address, phone across every directory and profile. Boring, foundational, still a factor.

Build a Page for Every Treatment You Want to Sell

The single highest-ROI move in dermatology SEO: a dedicated, genuinely useful page for each cosmetic treatment: laser resurfacing, chemical peels, microneedling, PRP, injectables if you offer them. Each page covers what the treatment is, who it’s for, what a visit looks like, recovery, and pricing signals, with schema markup and a consult CTA. One page per treatment beats one “Cosmetic Services” page for the same reason a specialist beats a generalist: Google ranks depth, and patients trust it.

Then aim your content at the condition searches and link them to the treatment pages: “acne scar treatment options” feeds the microneedling and laser pages; “melasma explained” feeds peels. That cluster structure, educational pages lifting commercial ones, is exactly how this site you’re reading ranks, and it works even better with a physician’s name on it.

Your Unfair Advantage: The Board-Certified Byline

Google’s quality systems explicitly favor experience and expertise on health topics, and a board-certified dermatologist is the strongest E-E-A-T signal in aesthetics. Put the physician’s name, credentials, and photo on every treatment page and article. Add physician-reviewed bylines to condition content. A med spa can’t match that credential. It’s the one SEO asset your competitors down the street can’t buy, and it compounds in AI search too, where engines cite credentialed sources first.

Real Result

We built a Greater Toronto Area med spa a curated PRP hair-restoration offer and ran it on Meta while local SEO compounded underneath. A $1,000 ad budget brought in 100+ leads at roughly $10 each and about $12,500 in month-one revenue. Paid fills the calendar now; SEO is what keeps filling it for free in month six and beyond.

The Technical Floor (Quick, Then Move On)

  • Fast, mobile-first site, since most treatment searches happen on phones
  • Schema markup: MedicalBusiness/Physician, service pages, FAQs, breadcrumbs
  • Clean URL structure and internal links from content to treatment pages
  • No tracking pixels on patient portals or intake flows, a privacy exposure most practices don’t know they have

What to Expect, and When

Local pack improvements can show in weeks once the profile and reviews engine are running. Treatment pages typically take 3-6 months to rank in a normal market: faster where competitors are thin, slower in major metros. SEO is the compounding layer: it won’t fill next week’s schedule, but every month it works, the free-patient share of your calendar grows. For next week, paid handles it.
