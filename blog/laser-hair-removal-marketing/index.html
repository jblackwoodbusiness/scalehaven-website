Laser Hair Removal Marketing That Books Packages

By John Blackwood, Founder · July 25, 2026 · 9 min read

Laser hair removal is a package business pretending to be a treatment business. One session does almost nothing; six to eight sessions deliver the result. Which means your marketing has one real job: sell the plan, not the zap. Every clinic that struggles with laser marketing is selling single sessions against a competitor's $99 coupon; every clinic that wins is selling completed outcomes on a schedule.

The Offer: Areas and Outcomes, Not Sessions

  • Package-first pricing. "6-session underarm package" as the advertised unit — with single sessions priced to make the package obvious.
  • The area bundle. Underarms + bikini, legs + underarms — bigger baskets, better economics, and a bigger commitment that improves show rates.
  • The "smooth by summer" deadline. Laser needs 6+ months of sessions — which makes fall and winter your real selling season. Market the finish line, not the treatment.
  • Unlimited-touch-up guarantees. A maintenance promise beats a discount — it removes the "will it last?" objection without cutting price.
Real Result

We built a Greater Toronto Area med spa a curated PRP hair-restoration offer and ran it on Meta. A $1,000 ad budget brought in 100+ leads at roughly $10 each — 10 booked consultations, 5 closed packages, about $12,500 in month-one revenue. No procedure video existed, so we ran image ads. The named offer did the heavy lifting, not the production value. Laser is the same lesson with a calendar attached: a named package with a deadline (“smooth by summer”) out-converts a session discount — and fills the machine in the off-season when competitors go quiet.

Paid Ads: Own the Off-Season

Everyone advertises laser in April. The smart money runs hardest October through February, when CPLs drop and the "ready by summer" logic is strongest. On Meta, expect $10-25 CPLs with package-led creative; on Google, "laser hair removal [city]" and "laser hair removal cost" capture ready-to-book searchers at $20-45.

Creative that converts: smooth-skin lifestyle imagery (compliant, positive framing), provider explainers on how many sessions and why, price-transparency posts ("what a full package actually costs"), and patient testimonials about being done shaving — the emotional payoff is freedom, not hairlessness.

Utilization Math: The Machine Must Stay Busy

Laser economics are device economics — the machine's payment is fixed whether it fires or not. The numbers that matter:

  • Package price vs single-session — packages lock 6-8 visits of utilization per sale
  • Cost per package sold, not cost per lead — a $25 lead closing a $1,200 package is the actual unit
  • Off-peak fill — memberships and bundle upgrades to smooth the weekly schedule

Run your own numbers in our ROI calculator — laser's package tickets make the math generous when the calendar stays full, and brutal when it doesn't. (Buying the device is the easy part — see our equipment guide.)

The Follow-Up: Selling a 6-Visit Commitment Takes Touches

A package is a bigger yes than a facial — expect more leads to need nurturing. Instant text-back, a consult that includes patch-test scheduling (get them physically in the door), financing presented as monthly cost, and a nurture sequence for the researchers. Once enrolled, automated session reminders protect the revenue you already sold — a package patient who drifts after session three is margin walking out the door.
