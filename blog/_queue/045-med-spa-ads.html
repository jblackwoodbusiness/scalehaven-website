Med Spa Ads: What Actually Works

By John Blackwood, Founder · {{TODAY_HUMAN}} · 10 min read

After six years working inside med spas and running campaigns for clinics across the US and Canada, we can tell you that almost every med spa ad that fails, fails for the same two reasons — and neither of them is targeting, budget, or the algorithm. The ads that win get two things right: a really good offer and a really good creative. Everything else is optimization.

This guide breaks down what a med spa ad that actually books patients looks like in 2026 — with real campaign numbers, the creative hierarchy we use with clients, examples of what to run when a clinic has zero video content, and the mistakes that quietly burn most ad budgets.

The Only Two Levers That Matter

The offer is what you’re asking someone to want. Not “we do Botox” — a specific, named, curated package that pulls the right patient in: a “Neurotoxin New Client Offer,” a “Lip Refresh,” a named event. A named offer signals intention and quality. A discount signals a race to the bottom, and it attracts price shoppers who bounce the moment a competitor goes $2 cheaper.

The creative is why they believe you. And here is the thing most clinics get backwards: cold traffic — people who have never heard of you — doesn’t book companies. People book people. A stranger scrolling Instagram doesn’t want a stock photo of someone lying on a treatment chair under a logo. They want to see the experience they’re going to get: the clinic, the provider’s face, the hands that will actually do the procedure, the person they’ll meet at their consult.

Real Result

From a live client account, one 30-day window: a “Neurotoxin New Client Offer” campaign brought in 128 leads at $3.83 per lead from under $500 in spend, and a Glo2Facial campaign added 69 leads at $4.66 — about 200 qualified leads for roughly $810, every one routed straight to the clinic’s team the moment it landed.

Anatomy of a Med Spa Ad That Converts

  • A hook in the first two seconds — a face talking directly to camera, or the treatment visibly happening. Not a logo animation.
  • A named offer with a clear outcome — what it is, who it’s for, what changes for them.
  • Proof of the experience — the room, the equipment, the team. Show the visit before they book it.
  • A qualifying lead form — treatment interest, timeline, budget comfort. You want fewer, better leads: financially qualified people your team can actually book.
  • A thank-you screen that books — hot leads go straight to your existing booking calendar while their motivation is at its peak.

The Creative Hierarchy: What to Run, in Order

1. Provider-on-camera video (the gold standard)

Vertical reels of your injector or esthetician talking to camera — explaining the offer, walking through what a first visit feels like, answering the question everyone is silently asking. Then B-roll: the procedure being performed, the clinic, the team. This is the content that positions your providers as the experts and trusted authorities in your space — and authority is what breaks the cold-traffic barrier. It doesn’t need to be polished. It needs to be real: authenticity consistently outperforms production value in this niche.

2. Treatment-in-action video

No confident speaker? Film the treatment itself with a voiceover or captions. Body contouring machines working, laser passes, a peel application — treatments that perform on camera do half the selling for you.

Real Result

We built a Greater Toronto Area med spa a curated PRP hair-restoration offer and ran it on Meta. A $1,000 ad budget brought in 100+ leads at roughly $10 each — 10 booked consultations, 5 closed packages, about $12,500 in month-one revenue. No procedure video existed, so we ran image ads. The named offer did the heavy lifting, not the production value.

That campaign is the clearest demonstration of the hierarchy: the offer carried image-only creative to a 12.5× return. Video with real faces raises the ceiling — but the offer sets the floor.

What the Numbers Should Look Like

From live campaigns across aesthetic clinics in 2026: well-built lead campaigns for injectables and facials land between $3 and $12 per qualified lead, with named-offer campaigns at the low end. Event promotions (open houses, launch nights) run separately as traffic campaigns and should produce landing-page views for under $0.50. If you’re paying $30+ per lead — or getting $5 leads that never answer the phone — the problem is almost always the offer or the qualification, not the platform. Full breakdowns by treatment are in our med spa CPL benchmarks.

The Mistakes That Burn Budgets

  • Discount-first ads. “$10/unit Botox” attracts people shopping for $9/unit Botox. Build a named offer instead — same budget, completely different patient.
  • Faceless creative. Stock photos and logo-driven graphics ask a stranger to trust a company. Show the human and the space, and the trust is already half-built.
  • Unqualified lead forms. A name-and-email form maximizes lead count and minimizes booked consults. Qualifying questions filter for patients who can actually say yes.
  • Slow follow-up. The best ad in the world can’t save a lead nobody contacts for two days. Leads should reach your team the moment they land — speed to lead decides who wins the booking.
  • Judging ads on impressions. Reach and likes don’t pay rent. The only metrics that matter: cost per qualified lead, and cost per booked consultation.

Where Med Spa Ads Fit in the Bigger Picture

Meta (Facebook + Instagram) is where demand gets created — reels and image ads put your offer in front of people who weren’t searching yet. Google Ads captures the demand that already exists (“botox near me”), and SEO compounds underneath both.
