Plastic Surgery Statistics 2026: 45 Key Numbers

By John Blackwood, Founder · {{TODAY_HUMAN}} · 10 min read

Plastic surgery is the highest-ticket corner of the aesthetics industry and the one with the best public data. Professional societies publish annual procedure counts, fee surveys, and demographic breakdowns, which means a surgeon or practice manager can benchmark against real numbers rather than vendor claims.

We compiled 45 statistics across six categories: market size, US procedure volume, the most popular surgeries, global comparisons, costs, patient demographics, and marketing benchmarks. Figures come from the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), the International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery (ISAPS), The Aesthetic Society, market research firms, and aggregated campaign data from aesthetic marketing. Most society data reports the prior calendar year; where a figure is an estimate or projection, we say so.

Citing these statistics? Please link back to this page as the source.

Market Size & Growth

  1. The global cosmetic surgery market is valued at an estimated $70 to $75 billion in 2026, depending on the scope of procedures counted. (Grand View Research; Fortune Business Insights)
  2. Analysts project the global market to grow at roughly 8 to 10% per year through 2030. (Grand View Research)
  3. North America accounts for roughly a third of global cosmetic surgery revenue, the largest regional share. (Grand View Research)
  4. Americans spent more than $15 billion on aesthetic procedures in a single year, surgical and non-surgical combined, according to The Aesthetic Society’s spending surveys. (The Aesthetic Society)
  5. Surgical procedures generate the majority of aesthetic spending even though non-surgical procedures outnumber them roughly 15 to 1, because average surgical case values run 10 to 30 times higher. (ASPS; ScaleHaven analysis)
  6. The broader medical aesthetics market, which includes the non-surgical side, is estimated at more than $28 billion in the US alone. (Grand View Research; see our aesthetic industry statistics)
  7. Growth in surgical volume has been slower than non-surgical, in the low single digits per year in the US, while minimally invasive procedures have grown 5 to 7% annually. (ASPS Procedural Statistics)

US Procedure Volume

  1. ASPS member surgeons performed approximately 1.6 million cosmetic surgical procedures in the most recent reported year. (ASPS Procedural Statistics)
  2. The same report counted more than 25 million minimally invasive cosmetic procedures, led by neuromodulator injections. (ASPS)
  3. Neuromodulators (Botox, Dysport, Xeomin, Jeuveau, Daxxify) exceeded 9 million treatments in the US in a single year. (ASPS)
  4. Hyaluronic acid fillers exceeded 5 million treatments. (ASPS)
  5. Reconstructive procedures, including breast reconstruction and tumor removal, add roughly 1 million additional cases per year to plastic surgeons’ workload. (ASPS)
  6. Roughly 1 in 5 cosmetic surgical patients report having had a previous cosmetic procedure, surgical or non-surgical. (ASPS; The Aesthetic Society)
  7. Since the pandemic, surgeons have reported a sustained rise in facial procedures attributed to video-call self-awareness, with facelift and eyelid volume up double digits from pre-2020 levels. (ASPS member surveys)

The Most Popular Plastic Surgeries

  1. Liposuction is the most performed cosmetic surgery in the US, at roughly 350,000 procedures per year. (ASPS)
  2. Breast augmentation is second at roughly 300,000 procedures. (ASPS)
  3. Abdominoplasty (tummy tuck) runs around 170,000 procedures annually and has grown steadily with the rise of post-weight-loss body contouring. (ASPS)
  4. Breast lift procedures number roughly 145,000 per year. (ASPS)
  5. Eyelid surgery (blepharoplasty) accounts for roughly 115,000 procedures and is the leading facial surgery by volume. (ASPS)
  6. Rhinoplasty, facelift, and breast reduction round out the top eight, each in the tens of thousands annually. (ASPS)
  7. GLP-1 weight-loss medications are reshaping demand: surgeons report sharp increases in consultations for body contouring after weight loss, including arm lifts, thigh lifts, and facial volume restoration. (ASPS member surveys)
  8. Breast implant removal and exchange procedures have risen meaningfully over the past five years as patients revisit older implants. (ASPS)

Global Comparisons

  1. Worldwide, plastic surgeons performed an estimated 35 million aesthetic procedures in the most recent ISAPS global survey, surgical and non-surgical combined. (ISAPS Global Survey)
  2. Of those, roughly 15 to 16 million were surgical and 19 to 20 million non-surgical. (ISAPS)
  3. Liposuction is also the #1 surgical procedure globally, at more than 2 million cases per year. (ISAPS)
  4. Breast augmentation is second worldwide at roughly 1.9 million procedures. (ISAPS)
  5. The United States performs more aesthetic procedures than any other country, followed by Brazil, Japan, Mexico, and Germany. (ISAPS)
  6. South Korea has the highest number of cosmetic procedures per capita. (ISAPS; industry analyses)
  7. Global surgical volume has grown roughly 40% over the past four years in ISAPS reporting, one of the fastest expansions on record. (ISAPS)

Costs & Economics

ASPS publishes average surgeon fees, which exclude anesthesia, facility, and related costs. Total patient cost typically runs 1.5 to 2 times the surgeon fee.

  1. Average surgeon fee for breast augmentation: about $4,800 to $5,000. (ASPS)
  2. Average surgeon fee for liposuction: about $4,000, per area treated. (ASPS)
  3. Average surgeon fee for abdominoplasty: about $8,000 to $8,500. (ASPS)
  4. Average surgeon fee for rhinoplasty: about $7,600 to $8,000. (ASPS)
  5. Average surgeon fee for a facelift: about $11,000 to $12,000. (ASPS)
  6. Average surgeon fee for eyelid surgery: about $4,100 to $4,500. (ASPS)
  7. All-in patient prices for common procedures land between $5,000 and $30,000+, which is why a single booked surgical case can cover a practice’s entire monthly marketing budget. (ScaleHaven analysis)
  8. A large majority of cosmetic surgery patients use financing for at least part of the cost; practices that display financing options prominently report higher consult-to-surgery conversion. (CareCredit; industry surveys)

Patient Demographics

  1. Women account for roughly 92 to 94% of cosmetic surgical procedures in the US. (ASPS)
  2. Men account for the remaining 6 to 8%, with gynecomastia surgery, liposuction, and eyelid surgery the most common male procedures. (ASPS)
  3. Patients aged 40 to 54 make up the largest share of cosmetic surgery, roughly 40% of procedures. (ASPS)
  4. Patients aged 30 to 39 are the second-largest group and the fastest-growing surgical segment. (ASPS)
  5. Patients 55 and older account for roughly a quarter of procedures, concentrated in facelift, eyelid, and neck lift. (ASPS)

Marketing & Patient Acquisition Benchmarks

  1. About 74% of aesthetic patients research online before booking a consultation, and 83% read Google reviews before choosing a provider. (RealSelf; BrightLocal)
  2. Cost per lead for plastic surgery on Google Ads typically runs $60 to $150, with consult-to-surgery close rates of 25 to 35% at well-run practices. (aggregated campaign data; see our cost per lead benchmarks)
  3. Surgical patients research for weeks, and the practice that responds first and follows up most consistently wins a disproportionate share of cases. Practices that respond within five minutes convert leads at several times the rate of those responding the next day. (lead-response research; ScaleHaven client data)
Real Result

For a Texas med spa we ran Google and Meta together from day one, with named offers and every lead routed straight to the front desk, who called it back. $2,500 in ad spend came back as $28,000 in first-visit revenue in the first 30 days, about $50 to acquire each new patient. No new software, no integrations: the leads landed in the booking system the clinic already used.

Methodology & Sources

Figures are compiled from the ASPS Procedural Statistics reports, ISAPS Global Survey results, The Aesthetic Society’s procedural and spending reports, market research from Grand View Research and Fortune Business Insights, consumer research from RealSelf and BrightLocal, and aggregated campaign performance from aesthetic marketing accounts. Society counts reflect member-surgeon reporting and the most recent published year; market values are analyst estimates and vary by scope. Ranges indicate variation across sources or years. Campaign benchmarks are directional, not guarantees. This page is updated as new annual reports publish.
