Best Med Spa Software in 2026: 10 Platforms Compared

By John Blackwood, Founder · {{TODAY_HUMAN}} · 11 min read

Disclosure first: ScaleHaven is a marketing agency, not a software company, and we do not take affiliate commissions from anything on this list. We have a different bias worth knowing about. Our whole model is zero integration: the patients we generate book straight into whatever system a clinic already uses, so we have worked inside most of the platforms below, from the client’s side, without a sales relationship with any of them. That is the lens here: which med spa software actually runs a clinic well, and which one quietly makes marketing harder.

“Best” depends on your size and whether you need a true medical record or a great front desk. So the list is organized by who each platform is best for, with the trade-offs stated plainly.

How to Judge Med Spa Software

  • Online booking that patients actually finish. Mobile-first, deposit capture, real-time availability, and a booking page you can link to from ads and your Google Business Profile. This one feature decides more revenue than everything else combined.
  • Charting, consents, and photos. Injectable charting with face maps, digital consents, before-and-after storage. Medical practices need a real EMR; a solo esthetician may not.
  • HIPAA. A signed Business Associate Agreement, access controls, audit logs. If a vendor hesitates on the BAA, walk.
  • Memberships and packages. Recurring billing, banked credits, package tracking. Retention revenue lives here.
  • Marketing tools and integrations. Two-way texting, email, review requests, and an API or Zapier connection so leads from ads can land in the system automatically.
  • Pricing model and exit cost. Per-provider seats versus flat plans, contract length, and above all: can you export your patient records and photos if you leave?

The 10 Best Med Spa Software Platforms

1. Boulevard

Best for: premium single and multi-location med spas that care about the client booking experience.

Boulevard started in high-end salons and has built a strong med spa product on the same foundation: an unusually smooth booking flow, precision scheduling that fills gaps, memberships, and clean payments. Charting and consents are solid for injectables and aesthetics. The trade-off is price; it is one of the more expensive options and is built for clinics that treat the front desk as a revenue center.

2. Zenoti

Best for: multi-location groups and franchises.

Zenoti is enterprise software: centralized reporting across locations, inventory, payroll, memberships, and a mobile app for guests. It is what many franchise med spa brands run on. For a single-location clinic it is more system than you need, and implementation is a project, not an afternoon.

3. Mangomint

Best for: growing single-location med spas that want modern software without enterprise complexity.

Mangomint is the platform most owners fall for on the demo: clean interface, excellent booking, express checkout, memberships, and thoughtful automation. Medical charting has matured but is still lighter than a dedicated aesthetics EMR. If your practice is more spa than clinic, it is a top-two choice.

4. AestheticsPro

Best for: med spas that want an all-in-one built specifically for aesthetics.

AestheticsPro has been in med spas for years and covers the full workflow: scheduling, EMR with charting and consents, photos, inventory, memberships, e-commerce, and marketing tools. It is purpose-built, which is its strength; the interface shows its age in places, and the marketing modules are functional rather than best-in-class.

5. PatientNow

Best for: practices that want EMR and marketing from one vendor with hands-on support.

PatientNow pairs a mature aesthetics EMR with a marketing suite and a services arm. Strong for injector-led practices and plastic surgery, with deep charting and photo management. Evaluate the bundle honestly: when EMR and marketing share a vendor, changing one means changing both.

6. Aesthetic Record

Best for: injector-led clinics that need serious charting and photo documentation.

Aesthetic Record is an EMR first: injectable mapping, standardized before-and-after capture, consents, e-prescribing and telehealth in supported states, plus booking and payments. Owners who prioritize clinical documentation and good-faith exams rank it highly. It is less polished on the retail and membership side than Boulevard or Mangomint.

7. Vagaro

Best for: budget-conscious med spas and those coming from the beauty side.

Vagaro is affordable, widely used, and includes a consumer marketplace that can send you bookings. It now offers HIPAA-compliant forms and medical add-ons. It is not a full EMR, and clinics with complex clinical workflows outgrow it. For a new med spa watching every dollar, it is a legitimate starting point.

8. Jane App

Best for: Canadian clinics and multi-disciplinary practices adding aesthetics.

Jane is a favorite across Canadian allied-health clinics for scheduling, charting, and billing, and it works well for aesthetics practices that also run other services. Charting is flexible and consents are easy. It lacks aesthetics-specific features like injectable face maps out of the box, and its marketing tooling is basic.

9. GlossGenius

Best for: solo injectors and independent estheticians.

Beautiful booking sites, payments, and a mobile-first workflow at a low monthly price. GlossGenius has added forms and HIPAA-aware features for aesthetics. It is a one-provider tool; the moment you hire a second injector and need shared charting, you will move up this list.

10. Nextech

Best for: dermatology and plastic surgery practices where the med spa is one department.

Nextech is a specialty EMR and practice management system for dermatology, plastic surgery, and ophthalmology, with an aesthetics module. If a surgeon or dermatologist owns the clinic and needs medical billing alongside cosmetic services, it belongs on the shortlist. Standalone med spas will find it heavier and pricier than they need.

What No Software Fixes

Every platform above will tell you it “grows your business.” None of them generates a new patient. Software stores, schedules, charts, and reminds. Demand comes from somewhere else, and the clinics that struggle usually have a software problem they think is a marketing problem, or the reverse. Two honest checks: if your calendar has empty slots, that is a lead generation problem no upgrade solves. If leads come in and sit unanswered for hours, that is a follow-up problem the fanciest booking page will not fix.

The best combination we see is boring: a booking platform your staff actually likes, a qualifying lead form on your ads, leads routed to the front desk the moment they land, and a person who calls back inside five minutes.

Real Result

For a Texas med spa we ran Google and Meta together from day one, with named offers and every lead routed straight to the front desk, who called it back. $2,500 in ad spend came back as $28,000 in first-visit revenue in the first 30 days, about $50 to acquire each new patient. No new software, no integrations: the leads landed in the booking system the clinic already used.

Switching? Read This First

Migrations fail on data, not features. Before you sign anything, get in writing what exports you receive if you leave: patient records, consents, photos, membership balances, and gift card liabilities. Plan the switch for your slowest month, run both systems in parallel for two weeks, and never migrate the week a marketing campaign launches. Your CRM and follow-up tools are a separate decision from your booking and EMR; we cover that side of the stack in our guide to the best CRM for med spas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an EMR or just booking software?

If licensed providers perform medical treatments (injectables, lasers, prescriptions), you need charting, consents, and a HIPAA-compliant record, which means an EMR or a booking platform with a real medical module. A solo esthetician doing facials can run on booking software with digital intake forms.

Is med spa software HIPAA compliant?

The software can support compliance, but compliance is yours. Confirm the vendor signs a Business Associate Agreement, then handle access controls, staff training, and consent workflows on your side. Vendors that will not sign a BAA should not hold patient data.

What is the best med spa software for a solo injector?

GlossGenius or Vagaro for booking and payments on a budget, Aesthetic Record if clinical documentation matters more than retail polish. Move to Mangomint, Boulevard, or AestheticsPro when you add a second provider and need shared charting.
