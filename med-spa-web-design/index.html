What Good Looks Like

A Site That Looks Good vs. One That Books

Most med spa websites are beautiful and broken. They win on photography and lose on the six things below, which are what actually decide whether a visitor becomes a booked patient.

01

A page for every treatment

One "Services" page listing twenty treatments ranks for nothing and answers nobody. Botox, Morpheus8, CoolSculpting, and laser hair removal each need their own page with candidacy, pricing, downtime, and FAQs. This is the single biggest structural difference between clinics that get found and clinics that do not.

02

Booking on every screen

Not one "Contact Us" tab in the navigation. A visible booking action on every treatment page, a sticky mobile bar, and a form short enough to finish in twenty seconds. Every extra field you add drops completion.

03

Real faces, not stock photography

Your providers, your treatment rooms, your actual results. Patients choose people, not logos. A page of models in white robes tells a visitor you have nothing real to show, and they can spot the stock library instantly.

04

Load time under two seconds

Aesthetic sites are image-heavy, and image-heavy usually means slow. Compress to WebP, lazy-load below the fold, and skip the page builders that ship a megabyte of scripts. A four-second page loses roughly a third of its visitors before it renders.

05

Proof where the decision happens

Reviews and before-and-afters belong on the treatment page a patient is reading, not parked on a separate testimonials tab nobody opens. A Botox review next to your Botox pricing does work that a homepage carousel never will.

06

Named offers, not a price list

A page that says "Botox: $12 a unit" invites comparison shopping. A page that sells a named first-visit package sells an outcome. The offer belongs in the page structure, not in a banner you swap out each month.
